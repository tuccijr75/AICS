# AICS coaching and physics directive

**Status:** current AICS doctrine, defined after the v1.9.5 release. This must be implemented and audited in the rebuilt system; it must not be falsely marked as already certified v1.9.5 behavior.

## Physics-first governing model

Every technique, transition, drill, and fight-IQ decision is evaluated for:

- kinetic-chain efficiency
- momentum creation, transfer, preservation, redirection, and controlled dissipation
- center-of-mass control
- leverage and torque
- force-vector alignment
- rotational inertia management
- base-of-support manipulation
- elastic recoil / stretch-shortening use where appropriate
- minimum-motion recovery
- counter exposure and failure recovery
- reaction-state transitions

"Momentum conservation" must not be taught as if the fighter were a closed system. The floor, opponent, cage, and gravity create external impulses.

"Lowest friction" means minimizing **internal mechanical waste**: unnecessary co-contraction, braking, extra steps, stance repair, excessive chambering, avoidable direction reversals, overrotation, and movement that adds no tactical value. It does **not** mean reducing useful foot-floor traction.

### Optimization principle

> **Maximum useful tactical effect per unit of unnecessary work and recovery cost.**

The terminal geometry of one action should become the starting geometry of the next valid action whenever tactically appropriate.

## Multidisciplinary doctrine

AICS remains multidisciplinary.

Source disciplines retain their identities. AICS selects mechanically and tactically valid solutions from across disciplines rather than preserving stylistic purity for its own sake.

The question is not "Which style owns this answer?" The question is "Which verified solution best solves this state for this athlete, ruleset, range, and opponent?"

## Coaching operating directive

AICS coaching should be mechanics-first and diagnostic, but athlete movement time must exceed coach exposition.

Default teaching cadence:

1. explain the problem once
2. demonstrate once or twice
3. athlete performs **3–5 reps**
4. coach gives **one primary mechanical correction**
5. athlete immediately retests
6. only then add another variable

Do not stack five corrections onto one repetition unless an immediate safety issue requires it.

## Decision training progression

Technique teaching progresses through:

1. clean mechanics
2. two-choice reaction
3. three-choice reaction
4. constrained resistance round
5. live recognition under appropriate resistance

The opponent's state selects the branch. Avoid fixed A→B→C choreography when resistance changes the geometry.

## Physics-aware cueing

Prefer cues that expose the physical problem:

- "Where is his weight?"
- "Which post can he no longer use?"
- "Where is your center relative to your base?"
- "Which force direction makes him step?"
- "What changed when he resisted?"
- "Can you recover without repairing your stance?"

Useful language includes COM, base/support polygon, posts, traction, ground reaction, impulse, torque, leverage, kinetic chain, momentum, and recovery cost—but coaching language should remain concise enough to produce movement rather than lectures.

## Drill contract

Every drill should define at least one observable success condition.

Examples:

- Level change: COM lowers through coordinated ankle/knee/hip action while the athlete remains immediately direction-change capable.
- Sprawl/TDD: attacker loses clean hip/leg connection and defender returns to usable base/inside control.
- Side-control escape: structural frame creates usable space before hip translation.
- Jab: target is reached and the hand/body recover without a stance-repair step.

## Resistance-state requirement

A Physics Card is not fully certified merely because its unopposed mechanics are correct.

Technique certification under resistance should model at minimum:

- clean execution
- opponent yields
- opponent braces
- opponent redirects
- opponent counters
- technique jams
- partial success
- cage/boundary constraint where relevant
- anthropometric mismatch
- fatigue-related mechanical degradation

Each branch should be representable as:

**trigger → opponent state → BOS → COM → available posts → force vector → kinetic chain → leverage/torque → counter exposure → minimum-motion recovery**

## Known migration note

Earlier Physics Card work established useful universal physics laws, but a deeper audit found that reaction-state modeling was still too templated across many cards. The rebuild should keep the universal laws while making opponent-response physics genuinely technique-specific.

Do not claim all 97 AICS Method techniques are resistance-certified until that individual card-by-card gate is completed.
